1A Note On Framing

Realistic and phased, not sweeping

The strongest research on African cross-border data policy points toward harmonisation, stronger institutions, clearer standards, and better infrastructure as the practical path forward — reached gradually, not through one sweeping reform. A country moving toward interoperable rules starting with a stronger national framework is a realistic recommendation; asking every country to adopt identical rules immediately is not. The recommendations below are written the first way.

For Policymakers

1

Align with AU & AfCFTA principles

Ground national reforms in the existing Malabo Convention and AU Data Policy Framework, rather than designing in isolation.

2

Improve DPA capacity

Invest in staffing and technical capability at data protection authorities so enforcement can keep pace with the law on paper.

3

Review localisation barriers

Rather than removing localisation rules outright, review them to ensure they're targeted, proportionate, and compatible with digital trade and security needs.

4

Publish clearer compliance guidance

Template contract clauses and transfer-assessment checklists let smaller organisations comply without specialist international legal counsel.

5

Coordinate on technical standards

Work with regional and continental bodies on shared technical standards, so compliance in one country doesn't mean rebuilding from scratch in the next.

For Investors & Developers

1

Choose reliable power and fibre

Weight site selection toward markets and locations with the strongest realistic power and connectivity access, not just the lowest land cost.

2

Phase development

Build capacity in stages that match realistic demand growth, rather than committing to full capacity — and full cost — on day one.

3

Design for the right IT load

Size systems against actual, realistic demand projections rather than best-case hyperscale assumptions.

4

Build compliance in from day one

Treat cross-border data policy as a design input for the project, not a legal review to complete just before launch.

For Communities & Civil Society

1

Require transparency

Push for site-selection and environmental review information to be genuinely public, not just technically available on request.

2

Require real consultation

Advocate for consultation processes that happen early enough to actually influence siting decisions, not after they've effectively been made.

3

Ask for ongoing impact reporting

Push for monitoring commitments that continue after construction, not just a one-time environmental impact assessment at approval.

For Regional Institutions

1

Support mutual recognition

Build mechanisms so one country's approved transfer safeguards can be recognised by its neighbours, rather than requiring separate approval everywhere.

2

Coordinate under AfCFTA

Use the African Continental Free Trade Area's digital trade protocol discussions as a practical vehicle for cross-border data alignment.

3

Prioritise, don't mandate uniformity

Support countries in strengthening national frameworks first, with interoperability as the medium-term goal — not a same-day requirement.

A note on ambition: ideas like a continental data governance body or formal "data embassies" are worth discussing, but they're longer-term goals rather than near-term recommendations. Keeping that distinction clear is what keeps a recommendations list credible rather than aspirational.
