African Market Overview

A continent-wide view of a market that's growing fast, but still concentrated in a few hubs.

Home/African Market Overview
1The Big Picture

Fast growth, concentrated in a few hubs

Africa's data centre market was valued at roughly $2 billion in 2025, with forecasts putting it above $4 billion by the early 2030s — a strong growth rate, but from a small base. The continent still represents well under 1% of global installed data centre capacity. Growth is real, but uneven: a handful of markets are pulling well ahead of the rest.

2Who's Building

The current market landscape

South Africa — the established hub

Home to the continent's most carrier-dense colocation market and the first major hyperscaler campuses. It's now approaching capacity limits as power constraints intensify, even as it remains the benchmark for the rest of the continent.

Nigeria & Kenya — the next tier

Both attracting significant capital on the back of rising enterprise demand and improving connectivity. Regional operators and international colocation providers have established a presence in both markets.

Ghana & secondary hubs

Smaller individually, but part of a "rest of Africa" segment showing strong momentum — alongside markets like Morocco, Ethiopia, and Rwanda — driven by rising digital adoption and supportive government policy.

Regional operators to know

Names that recur across country profiles: Teraco (South Africa's dominant carrier-neutral operator), Africa Data Centres, Vantage Data Centers, Raxio Data Centres, and MainOne (now part of Equinix) in Nigeria and Ghana.

3What Makes This Market Different

Slower absorption than mature hubs

In mature markets like Northern Virginia, Frankfurt, or London, new facilities are often pre-leased before they're even finished, with hyperscalers and enterprise tenants absorbing capacity fast. Outside South Africa, African markets see a slower, more gradual occupancy ramp-up — meaning disciplined, phased investment tends to outperform speculative overcapacity build-outs. That reality shapes the site-selection and investment guidance throughout this site.
