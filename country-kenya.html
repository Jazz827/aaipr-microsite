1Market Maturity

Where the market stands today

Kenya is consistently named among Africa's leading data centre markets, with Nairobi serving as the primary hub for East Africa. It has attracted growing hyperscale cloud provider interest, positioning it as the region's default choice for enterprises needing local East African capacity.

2Infrastructure Readiness

Power, fibre, and physical constraints

Infrastructure is comparatively strong for the region, with improving fibre connectivity and grid capacity, though — as in most African markets outside South Africa — reliability and backup power planning remain a real part of any project's cost base.

3Policy & Regulatory Context

The legal environment

Regulated under the Data Protection Act, 2019 and the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC), whose sections 48–49 restrict cross-border transfer to countries without adequate safeguards, consent, or contractual protections. The ODPC has also published standalone guidance notes detailing acceptable transfer mechanisms.

4Demand Drivers

What's fuelling growth

A strong technology and fintech sector (Kenya's mobile money ecosystem is globally referenced), a well-educated technical workforce, and East Africa's broader demand for regional cloud and colocation capacity.

5Connectivity

Fibre and network access

Nairobi is well served by regional fibre routes and improving submarine cable access via the Kenyan coast, reinforcing its role as an East African connectivity hub.

6Investment Relevance

What this means for investors and developers

A relatively lower-risk East African entry point, particularly for hyperscale and enterprise cloud investment, backed by a maturing regulatory regime and a demonstrated track record of technology-sector demand.
