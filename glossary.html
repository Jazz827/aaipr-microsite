Glossary of Terms

Plain-language definitions for every legal, technical, and environmental term used across this microsite.

Adequacy (Adequacy Determination)
An official finding that another country's data protection standards are strong enough that transfers there can happen with fewer extra legal steps.
Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs)
An internal data-protection rulebook, approved by a regulator, that lets one company move data safely between its own offices in different countries.
Cloud Service
Storage and computing power offered on demand by a provider, rather than run on a company's own hardware. Choosing a cloud provider often decides which country your data physically lives in.
Consent
A legal basis for transfer where the individual is clearly informed and agrees to their data being sent abroad — typically used for smaller or one-off transfers rather than routine business operations.
Cooling Constraints
The difficulty and expense of keeping data-centre equipment at a safe operating temperature — a particular challenge in hot climates, and a real limit on how large a facility can grow.
Cross-Border Data Transfer
Sending, storing, or accessing personal data outside the country where it was originally collected. See: Cross-Border Data Transfers.
Data Centre
A physical facility built to store, process, and secure digital information — the buildings behind cloud platforms, banking systems, government services, and AI tools. See: Data Infrastructure.
Data Sovereignty
The principle that data is subject to the laws of the country in which it is collected or stored — closely related to, but narrower than, digital sovereignty.
Derogation
A narrow, one-off legal exception allowing a transfer without the usual safeguards — for example, to complete a contract or handle a legal claim. Meant to be rare, not routine.
Digital Sovereignty
A country's ability to control its own data, systems, and digital infrastructure, rather than depending on foreign laws, providers, or facilities. See: The Connection Between Them.
Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)
A formal study of how a proposed development — such as a data centre — would affect land, water, air, and surrounding communities, typically required before construction can be approved.
Fibre Network
Cabling — often running under the sea or across countries — that carries data at high speed between cities, data centres, and continents.
Harmonisation
The process of aligning laws or standards across different countries so they work together consistently, rather than each jurisdiction operating on incompatible rules.
Interoperability
The ability of different countries' legal or technical systems to work together smoothly — for example, one country's approved transfer mechanism being recognised by another.
Internet Exchange Point (IXP)
A physical location where different networks connect directly to each other, so local data doesn't have to take an expensive detour through an international route.
Localisation (Data Localisation)
A legal requirement that certain data be stored or processed within a specific country's borders, rather than transferred abroad.
Low-Carbon Design
Designing infrastructure — like a data centre — from the outset to minimise its carbon footprint, through renewable energy integration, efficient cooling, and material choices.
Power Supply
Reliable electricity is one of the biggest factors in where new data-centre capacity gets built. Unstable supply means backup generation, higher costs, and less resilience.
Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE)
A standard industry metric measuring how efficiently a data centre uses energy — the ratio of total facility power to power actually used by computing equipment. Lower is better.
Regulatory Framework
The combined set of laws, rules, and guidance issued by a government or regulator that together govern how an activity — such as data processing — must be carried out.
Resilience
The capacity of a system — legal, infrastructural, or environmental — to keep functioning, or recover quickly, when disrupted by failures, shocks, or changing conditions.
Safeguards
The protective measures — legal, technical, or organisational — put in place to ensure personal data stays secure and is used only as intended, especially once it crosses a border.
Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)
Pre-approved contract terms that legally bind the sender and receiver of data to protect it, used when there's no adequacy determination in place.
Sensitive Data
Personal data that carries extra risk if exposed — health records, financial details, biometric data, and similar categories. Usually held to a higher legal standard than ordinary personal data.
Sustainable Cooling
Cooling approaches for data centres — such as using outside air or low-water systems — designed to minimise water and energy use compared to conventional mechanical cooling.
Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE)
A standard industry metric measuring how much water a data centre uses relative to the energy it consumes — a key indicator in water-stressed regions.
