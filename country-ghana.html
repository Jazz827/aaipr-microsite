Ghana

A smaller market with real momentum, gaining ground as a secondary West African hub.

1Market Maturity

Where the market stands today

Ghana sits in the 'rest of Africa' tier by scale — smaller individually than South Africa, Nigeria, or Kenya — but is showing strong momentum as a secondary hub, with MainOne (now Equinix) among the operators providing colocation and connectivity capacity in Accra.

2Infrastructure Readiness

Power, fibre, and physical constraints

As a smaller market, infrastructure is less built-out than the three larger profiles here, though improving connectivity and government-led digital transformation initiatives are steadily narrowing the gap. Power and cooling constraints common across the region apply here too, and should be budgeted for accordingly.

3Policy & Regulatory Context

The legal environment

Governed by the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843) and Ghana's Data Protection Commission. Notably, the Act's cross-border transfer provisions are limited compared to more detailed regimes like South Africa's or Kenya's — creating some legal uncertainty for businesses that a more detailed regulatory update could resolve.

4Demand Drivers

What's fuelling growth

Rising enterprise digitalisation, government-backed digital transformation initiatives, and growing regional demand for hosting closer to West African users than a Nigeria- or Europe-based alternative.

5Connectivity

Fibre and network access

Accra has growing regional fibre and submarine cable connectivity, though on a smaller scale than Lagos — positioning Ghana as a complement to, rather than a competitor with, Nigeria's West African hub role.

6Investment Relevance

What this means for investors and developers

A higher-risk, higher-upside profile than the other three markets here: real momentum and government support, but thinner existing infrastructure and less regulatory specificity — better suited to phased entry than a first large commitment.
