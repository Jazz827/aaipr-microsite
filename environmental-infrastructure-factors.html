Environmental Dimension

Environmental & Infrastructure Factors

Power, cooling, water, land, and climate risk — the physical constraints that shape every data-centre build.

Home/Environmental & Infrastructure Factors
1Overview

The physical constraints behind every site decision

The factors on this page aren't just technical problems — they're environmental ones too, and increasingly they're treated as site-selection criteria from day one rather than a retrofit. Every item below also appears in the Site Selection Scorecard.

2The Factors, In Detail

Tap each factor to expand it

01Power availability
A stable power supply is one of the biggest factors determining where new capacity gets built. Outside South Africa, grid reliability often runs below 60%, pushing operators toward continuous rather than standby backup generation — and toward diesel budgets that can reach 55-65% of operating expense in the most power-constrained markets.
02Cooling
Cooling constraints — the difficulty and expense of keeping equipment at a safe operating temperature in hot climates — directly limit facility size and cost. Sustainable cooling approaches, like free-air cooling where climate allows, reduce both water and energy demand.
03Water stress
Many cooling systems rely on water, which competes with agricultural and municipal needs in water-stressed regions. Water usage effectiveness is an increasingly standard metric for tracking this alongside energy use.
04Connectivity
Proximity to fibre routes and internet exchange points reduces both latency and the need for additional infrastructure build-out — which itself carries a cost and environmental footprint.
05Cost
Construction and equipment are often imported and priced in foreign currency, making African builds disproportionately expensive relative to local costs of capital and operating margins.
06Land & zoning
Suitable land needs the right zoning, proximity to power and fibre, and clear title — often a slower, less predictable process than the technical build itself.
07Technical skills
Specialised roles — data-centre engineers, network operators, cooling technicians — are in short supply in several markets, slowing both construction and ongoing operations.
08Environmental exposure & climate risk
Flood risk, extreme heat, and longer-term climate volatility all affect where a facility can safely and sustainably operate over its full lifetime — not just at the point of construction.
3Building Responsibly

Sustainability practices worth budgeting for

Low-carbon design

Design for renewable power integration from the outset, and track power usage effectiveness as a standard operating metric, not an occasional audit.

Environmental impact assessment

Treat a full environmental impact assessment as a standard pre-construction step covering land, water, air, and community impact — not just a permitting formality.

Lifecycle planning

Plan for the facility's full lifecycle — construction, operation, equipment refresh, and eventual decommissioning — including responsible disposal or recycling of hardware.

Resilience

Design for the climate risks identified during site selection, not just current conditions — a facility built for today's climate may face a different risk profile within its operating lifetime.

Practical Summary A facility built without environmental planning tends to cost more, run less reliably, and face more regulatory friction over its lifetime than one designed around its site's real constraints from the start. This isn't a cost added on top of infrastructure planning — it is infrastructure planning.
