Why Data Centres Matter

The role data centres play in cloud services, digital trade, connectivity, and economic growth.

Home/Why Data Centres Matter
1The Basic Role

The buildings behind everything digital

A data centre is a physical facility built to store, process, and secure digital information. Every cloud app, banking system, government service, and AI tool people use is ultimately running on servers sitting inside one of these buildings — somewhere. Where that "somewhere" is has real consequences for cost, speed, control, and resilience.

2Four Reasons This Matters

What data centres actually enable

Cloud services

Cloud platforms are, physically, data centres rented out as a service. Local capacity means local businesses can build on cloud infrastructure without routing everything through a facility on another continent.

Digital trade

E-commerce, digital payments, and cross-border services all depend on infrastructure that can move and process data reliably — data centres are the backbone that makes digital trade physically possible.

Regional connectivity

A well-placed data centre, connected to fibre and an internet exchange point, reduces how far local data has to travel — improving speed and reducing dependence on expensive international routes.

Economic growth & digital resilience

Local infrastructure supports local jobs, local hosting for local businesses, and continuity when international links are disrupted — a resilience dividend, not just a convenience.

3A Growing, Uneven Market

Fast growth, from a small base

Africa's data centre market is expanding quickly — industry estimates put it growing from roughly $2 billion toward $4+ billion in installed value by the early 2030s. But the continent still represents well under 1% of global installed data centre capacity, and growth is concentrated in a handful of hubs.
