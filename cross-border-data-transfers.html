Concept 01

Cross-Border Data Transfers

What it means for personal data to leave a country — and the legal safeguards Nigeria and Kenya put in its way.

Home/Cross-Border Data Transfers
1Definition

Moving data across a border, legally

A cross-border data transfer happens whenever personal data is sent, stored, or accessed outside the country it was collected in — a cloud backup, a customer-support ticket routed overseas, or an AI model processing user data on a foreign server all count. It is a routine, essential part of modern digital life: businesses use international cloud platforms, researchers collaborate across countries, and digital services need to reach users wherever they are.

But routine doesn't mean unregulated. Because personal data can reveal sensitive things about real people, most countries — Nigeria and Kenya included — only allow it to leave under specific conditions.

2Legal Safeguards

What has to be true before data can leave

Data protection laws typically require one of the following before a transfer is allowed:

Adequacy determination

The receiving country is officially recognised as having strong-enough data protection standards, so transfers can happen with fewer extra steps.

Standard contractual clauses (SCCs)

Pre-approved contract terms between the sender and receiver that legally bind both sides to protect the data, even without an adequacy finding.

Binding corporate rules (BCRs)

An internal rulebook, approved by a regulator, that lets a single company move data safely between its own offices in different countries.

Consent

The individual is clearly informed and agrees to their data being sent abroad — used mainly for smaller, one-off transfers.

Some laws also allow narrow derogations — one-off exceptions for things like completing a contract or handling a legal claim — but these are meant to be exceptions, not a routine workaround.

3Sensitive Data

Some data faces a higher bar

Health records, financial details, biometric data, and other sensitive data carry a higher risk if mishandled — so both Nigeria and Kenya apply stricter compliance requirements before this kind of data can cross a border. In practice, that often means extra safeguards, more documentation, or in some cases requiring a regulator to review the transfer method before it's used.

4Nigeria

The Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023

Nigeria

NDPA 2023 & the NDPC

Personal data is regulated through the Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023, and guidance from the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC) — which replaced the earlier NDPR framework. Cross-border transfers are permitted, but only when legal conditions and privacy safeguards are met.

Sensitive data faces stronger compliance requirements, and the NDPC has continued to issue guidance clarifying how organisations should assess and document a transfer before it happens.

5Kenya

The Data Protection Act, 2019

Kenya

DPA 2019 & the ODPC

Personal data is regulated through the Data Protection Act, 2019, and guidance from the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC). Cross-border transfers are also allowed, but only with appropriate safeguards, lawful grounds, or consent where required.

Sensitive data is treated with extra caution, and the ODPC has published guidance notes on cross-border transfers and data sharing to help organisations apply the law consistently.
