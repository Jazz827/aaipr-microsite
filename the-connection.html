The Connection Between Them

Data law and data infrastructure aren't two separate stories — they're two halves of the same one.

Home/The Connection Between Them
1The Core Idea

Two questions, one answer

Cross-border data rules decide where data may go. Data infrastructure decides where data can actually stay. Treat them as separate topics and you miss the point — treat them together, and Africa's digital-governance picture snaps into focus.

2Why They Depend On Each Other

If a country wants to keep data local

If a country wants sensitive data to remain within its borders, it needs the facilities to host it — the data centres, power, and cooling capacity to actually keep that data local. A law requiring local storage means nothing without somewhere to store it.

If a country wants data to move across borders securely, it needs both legal safeguards and reliable infrastructure — connections fast and stable enough that the safeguards can actually be enforced in practice, not just on paper.

3Six Places This Shows Up

What's actually at stake

Digital sovereignty

Digital sovereignty — a country's ability to control its own data, systems, and infrastructure — depends on both halves at once. Data hosted abroad is subject to foreign law; data hosted locally still needs local infrastructure to actually work.

Regional trade

Inconsistent transfer rules between neighbouring countries raise friction for cross-border commerce. Harmonised law only pays off if the infrastructure exists to move data at the speed trade requires.

Cloud dependence

Where local infrastructure is thin, organisations default to foreign cloud providers — which quietly shifts both data location and legal jurisdiction, regardless of what national law prefers.

AI

African AI systems need data, compute, and local infrastructure to work well in real-world settings. Useful AI for health, education, and public services needs sound governance and stronger infrastructure — together.

Trust

Users and businesses trust systems they believe are accountable. That trust depends on knowing both which rules apply and where the underlying infrastructure actually sits.

Enforceability

A safeguard is only as strong as a regulator's ability to check it. Weak local infrastructure — including within regulators themselves — makes even well-written law harder to enforce in practice.
