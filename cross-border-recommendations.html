Cross-Border Recommendations

Feasible next steps for closing the gap between national data laws and the African Union's harmonisation goals.

Home/Cross-Border Recommendations
1Starting Point

Closing the gap, deliberately

The country comparison makes the problem visible: African data protection laws vary widely in age, strictness, and detail, and continental efforts like the Malabo Convention and the AU Data Policy Framework are still unevenly adopted. The recommendations below are aimed at governments, regulators, and regional bodies working to close that gap — not at replacing national sovereignty over data policy, but at making it easier for good national laws to work well together.

2Seven Recommendations

What feasible progress looks like

1

Regional harmonisation

Align core definitions — what counts as personal data, sensitive data, and a valid transfer mechanism — across regional economic communities, so businesses aren't rebuilding compliance from scratch at every border.

2

Interoperability

Design national transfer mechanisms (adequacy processes, contractual clauses) to be mutually recognisable across countries, rather than each jurisdiction inventing its own incompatible version.

3

Safeguards for sensitive data

Keep the higher bar for health, financial, and biometric data consistent across countries, so sensitive-data protection doesn't become the weakest link in an otherwise harmonised system.

4

Easier compliance

Publish clear, practical guidance — template contract clauses, transfer-assessment checklists — so smaller organisations and public agencies can comply without needing specialist international legal counsel.

5

Regulatory cooperation

Give data protection authorities formal channels to coordinate on cross-border enforcement, echoing the Malabo Convention's call for cooperation between national regulators.

6

Capacity-building

Invest in staffing and technical capacity at newer or under-resourced data protection authorities, so enforcement keeps pace with the law on paper.

7

Balanced openness and protection

Treat openness and protection as complementary, not competing, goals — the AU Data Policy Framework's own premise is that trustworthy safeguards are what make wider data flows possible in the first place.
