Nigeria vs Kenya

Two countries, two data protection laws, and one shared logic: transfer is never automatic.

Home/Nigeria vs Kenya
1Side by Side

How each country regulates transfer

Nigeria

Data Protection Act, 2023

Personal data is regulated through the Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023, and guidance from the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC).

  • Cross-border transfers are allowed only when legal conditions and privacy safeguards are met.
  • Sensitive data faces stronger compliance requirements.
  • Built on the earlier NDPR framework, since formalised into law.
Kenya

Data Protection Act, 2019

Personal data is regulated through the Data Protection Act, 2019, and guidance from the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC).

  • Cross-border transfers are allowed only with appropriate safeguards, lawful grounds, or consent where required.
  • Sensitive data is treated with extra caution.
  • ODPC issues standalone guidance notes on cross-border transfers and data sharing.
Key Takeaway Both countries allow data to cross borders, but neither treats transfer as automatic. The law expects protection, oversight, and justification before personal data moves outside national borders.
2Where They Differ

Same logic, different regulator model

Nigeria's framework is newer and more centralised under a dedicated commission (the NDPC) established directly by statute. Kenya's regime has had a few more years to mature under the ODPC, with a growing body of standalone guidance notes that fill in practical detail the base law doesn't spell out. In both cases, the direction of travel is the same: tighter documentation requirements and closer regulatory attention to how — not just whether — data leaves the country.
