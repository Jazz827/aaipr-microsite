Environmental Recommendations for Data Centres

A practical guide to site selection and sustainability for data infrastructure built in Africa.

Home/Environmental Recommendations
1Why This Belongs Here

Sustainability is a site-selection question

The constraints facing African data infrastructure — power, cooling, water, environmental exposure — aren't just technical problems. They're also environmental ones. Choosing where and how to build a data centre well means treating sustainability as a design input from day one, not a retrofit.

2Site-Selection Criteria

Where to build

01Climate risk
Assess long-term temperature trends and extreme-heat frequency for the site, not just current averages — cooling demand and equipment lifespan both depend on it.
02Flood risk
Avoid floodplains and low-lying coastal areas prone to storm surge; a single flood event can take a facility offline for months and destroy irreplaceable equipment.
03Water availability
Evaluate local water stress before committing to water-intensive cooling. In water-scarce regions, a facility competing with agricultural or municipal supply is a liability, not just a cost.
04Energy reliability & renewable access
Prioritise sites with stable grid access and a realistic path to renewable power — solar and wind potential varies significantly by region and should shape the site decision, not just the operating budget.
05Connectivity
Proximity to fibre routes and internet exchange points reduces the need for additional infrastructure build-out, which itself carries an environmental footprint.
06Land use & environmental sensitivity
Avoid ecologically sensitive land and conduct a proper environmental impact assessment before committing — displacement of communities or ecosystems should weigh directly in the site decision.
07Regulatory compliance
Confirm zoning, environmental permitting, and any national environmental impact assessment requirements early — regulatory delays are one of the most common causes of stalled builds.
3Sustainability Practices

How to build and run it responsibly

Sustainable cooling

Favour low-water and free-cooling designs (using ambient outside air where climate allows) over conventional water-intensive systems, and track water usage effectiveness alongside energy metrics.

Low-carbon design

Design for renewable power integration from the outset, and track power usage effectiveness as a standard operating metric, not an occasional audit.

Environmental impact assessment

Treat a full environmental impact assessment as a standard pre-construction step, covering land, water, air, and community impact — not just a permitting formality.

Lifecycle planning

Plan for the facility's full lifecycle — construction, operation, equipment refresh, and eventual decommissioning — including responsible disposal or recycling of hardware.

Resilience

Design for the climate risks identified at the site-selection stage, not just current conditions — a facility built for today's climate may face a different risk profile within its operating lifetime.

Community & regulatory engagement

Maintain ongoing dialogue with local communities and regulators past initial approval — sustainability commitments are easiest to keep when they're monitored, not just promised at launch.

Practical Summary A data centre built without environmental planning tends to cost more, run less reliably, and face more regulatory friction over its lifetime than one designed around its site's real constraints from the start. Environmental planning isn't a cost added on top of infrastructure planning — it is infrastructure planning.
