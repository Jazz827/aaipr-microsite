1How To Use This

A practical evaluation framework

Choosing a data-centre site means weighing a fixed set of factors against each other — no single criterion decides it alone. The scorecard below groups the core siting criteria first, then the technical metrics that determine whether a promising site can actually support the intended IT load.

2Core Siting Criteria

What to evaluate first

Power reliability

Grid stability and available capacity — outside South Africa, grid reliability often runs below 60%, pushing facilities toward continuous rather than standby backup generation.

Core

Fibre connectivity

Proximity to fibre routes and an internet exchange point reduces both latency and the cost of building additional connectivity infrastructure.

Core

Land availability

Suitable land with clear title and appropriate zoning, ideally already close to power and fibre corridors.

Core

Water access

Availability for cooling systems, weighed against local water stress — a growing factor in site decisions, not an afterthought.

Core

Climate risk

Flood exposure, extreme heat frequency, and longer-term climate trends that affect cooling load and structural risk.

Core

Permitting

Zoning approval, environmental permitting, and utility interconnection timelines — often the least predictable part of a project schedule.

Core

Grid resilience

Redundant substations or feeders serving the site, reducing single points of failure in the power supply chain.

Core

Logistics

Road access for construction and equipment delivery, and proximity to a skilled labour pool for both build and operations.

Core

Expansion potential

Room to grow on the same site or campus — sizing land and power capacity for future phases, not just the first build.

Core
3Technical Metrics

What determines feasibility on paper

IT load

The actual power draw of computing equipment the site needs to support — the number that everything else (power, cooling) is sized against.

Technical

Redundancy

Backup capacity for power and cooling systems, described using industry tiers — see Tier III / Tier IV in the Glossary.

Technical

Utility interconnection

The technical and contractual process of connecting the facility to the grid at the required capacity — often a long-lead-time item.

Technical

MEP readiness

Mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems design — whether local contractors and supply chains can actually deliver and maintain what's specified.

Technical

Phased build-out

Building capacity in stages that match realistic demand growth, rather than committing to full capacity — and full cost — on day one.

Technical
How To Read This No African market outside South Africa currently scores "strong" across every core criterion — that's normal, not disqualifying. The scorecard is a way to compare trade-offs between sites and markets, not a pass/fail test. See Country Profiles for how these criteria play out in practice.
